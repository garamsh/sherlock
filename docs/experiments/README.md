# Experiments

Structured experiments that produced evidence for architectural decisions. Each subdirectory is one experiment: a report, its harness, and its raw results. An experiment record answers "why is this architecture the current one" with reproducible numbers; it is not a responsibility document (current state lives in `docs/architecture/`).

## Index

- `memory-architecture/` — the memory-store experiment behind ADR 0006's 2026-08-14 revision: 38 candidate store/retrieval architectures, four workloads, six recorded runs. Report, harness, and raw results included.
- `memory-cycle/` — the memory-cycle experiment: write path, read path, and store policy tested as one system. Established that the voluntary writeback is the root failure (0.00 context-recall) and that structured output is the minimal structural fix.
- `memory-retrieval/` — what the read path returns for a task statement, and what the choice of embedder costs it, measured against `internal/memory` itself rather than a reimplementation of it. Established that the shipped ranking returns 0.037 to 0.092 of the planted answers in its top ten while the same ranking over the same corpus returns 0.799 to 0.875 with salience and age held constant; that a real embedder is worth a great deal to relevance and nothing to what the read path returns; and that no supersession threshold separates corrections from coexisting facts on the shipped embedder either, where the mechanism retired every coexisting pair and 6 corrections in 250. That mechanism has since been removed (#529); the record's figures are pinned to what ran.
- `memory-frames/` — what a memory structure does with a decade of turns, measured through a seam several structurally different designs can sit behind. Builds the instrument the structure programme is compared on — a ten-year workload whose facts are carried, replaced, reverted and held true in parallel, reported per year and per class — and measures the shipped design through it as the baseline. Established that the shipped structure holds a fact for the whole span under a perfect writeback while returning a superseded value on most revised threads, that it scores at the bounded-document floor without one, and that what it stores on disk runs orders of magnitude above what it holds. Also holds the attribute-addressed frame: addressing a fact at `(subject, attribute)` removes the supersession failure outright and leaves arity undecided, which no rule reading the extracted triple recovers — the interval between writes and the attribute's own addresses both carry no signal, and the rule that scores best reads the wording and is a list that grows. And the remaining structures: validity intervals, an append-only log with a derived view, and the ceiling where the write states the operation. Established that three structurally different stores taking one arity decision answer identically at every year and class, so the store shape does not change the answer and the arity rule does; that what separates them is what they hold, what they read to answer one question, and what they can be asked; and that the shipped design and the derived view both read everything they hold on every question, growing with the decade. And what a wrong declaration costs the ceiling: the two error directions fail in opposite figures, a wrong join heals at the next replacement while a wrong replacement heals at nothing, and a real model — measured, not assumed — errs almost entirely on the wording that marks an addition with a qualifier rather than a conjunction, so the ceiling is not reached and what remains is a trade rather than a structure that removes both failures. And whether a structure can be built in which no write destroys: a store that closes a span instead of deleting keeps every wrongly-cleared value and answers identically to one that deletes them, because a present-tense question does not return a closed span, and reaching it would need a rule for when to reopen one — which is the arity decision arriving one step later. The record closes with what would be chosen today: the structure and the direction of error are settled by the evidence, and the policy for an unreliable declaration is not. And what a structure cannot reach it can never update: a write that computes its address corrects a fact no read ever returned, where a store that supersedes what its own lookup surfaces did not — so reading and updating are coupled in one design and decoupled in the other. Nothing is frozen on this workload, in any structure, because a decade of questions reaches everything each holds; the bytes that cannot be reached are the archive, and nothing approaches the capacity ceiling for more than a dozen decades. And what the structures do under constraint: at a capacity that bites they collapse together, because the eviction rule decides the outcome rather than the store, and the class a bound destroys first is the one asserted once and asked nine years later. A rule keyed on use saves that class where a rule keyed on age loses all of it — so forgetting is decidable from what a store has watched happen, where arity was not — and bounding the shipped design's index makes its footprint grow, because what it evicts it archives.
- `memory-creation/` — which structure creates, corrects and retires a memory entry. Established that no similarity threshold can decide supersession, that what the decider is shown decides the outcome (a retrieval offer scores 0.71 on corrections against a recency window's 0.01, the two differing in nothing else), and that key-addressed replacement relocates the failure rather than removing it.
